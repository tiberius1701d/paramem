## Brace-binding contract — REQUIRED for every NEW entity you mint

You may introduce new placeholders for entities the extractor missed (an event, topic, location, project, etc. named in the transcript). Pre-existing anonymizer placeholders stay BARE (`Person_1`, `City_1`); placeholders YOU mint must use BRACED form (`{{Event_1}}`).

Every new braced placeholder MUST appear in BOTH:
- a fact in `add` (or in a `modify` `fields` block), under subject or object.
- `bindings` — a dict entry `"Prefix_N": "<exact transcript span>"` (key WITHOUT braces).

Type-appropriate prefixes: Person, City, Country, Org, Event, Topic, Location, Thing, Role. Allocate the smallest free index above every value you can see — the input facts below and the transcript above are the whole of what you were shown, so counting both bare and braced occurrences there is always enough; you never need to guess an index used elsewhere.

POSITIVE — transcript: `"...Person_1 led the agile transformation initiative at Org_1..."`
→ add: `[{{"subject": "Person_1", "predicate": "led", "object": "{{Event_1}}", "relation_type": "factual", "confidence": 0.9}}, {{"subject": "Person_1", "predicate": "works_at", "object": "Org_1", "relation_type": "factual", "confidence": 0.9}}]`
→ bindings: `{{"Event_1": "the agile transformation initiative"}}`

POSITIVE (role-instance — bind co-temporal attributes to ONE entity, not flat triples on speaker0) — transcript: `"...I was Senior Engineer at Org_1 in City_1 from Jan 2014 to Sep 2017..."`
→ add: `[{{"subject": "speaker0", "predicate": "held_role", "object": "{{Role_1}}", "relation_type": "factual", "confidence": 0.9}}, {{"subject": "{{Role_1}}", "predicate": "at_company", "object": "Org_1", "relation_type": "factual", "confidence": 0.9}}, {{"subject": "{{Role_1}}", "predicate": "in_location", "object": "City_1", "relation_type": "factual", "confidence": 0.9}}, {{"subject": "{{Role_1}}", "predicate": "start_date", "object": "Jan 2014", "relation_type": "temporal", "confidence": 0.9}}, {{"subject": "{{Role_1}}", "predicate": "end_date", "object": "Sep 2017", "relation_type": "temporal", "confidence": 0.9}}]`
→ bindings: `{{"Role_1": "Senior Engineer"}}`

NEGATIVE — same transcript:
- WRONG: `add: [{{"subject":"Person_1","predicate":"led","object":"Project_1",...}}]` — bare `Project_1` looks like an anonymizer placeholder but isn't in the input mapping → the WHOLE delta is rejected (not just this one fact) and the pre-enrichment facts are kept unchanged.
- WRONG: `add: [{{"subject":"Person_1","predicate":"led","object":"{{Event_1}}",...}}]` with `Event_1` missing from `bindings` → no binding to substitute → the WHOLE delta is rejected.
- WRONG: `bindings: {{"{{Event_1}}": "..."}}` — key includes braces → lookup misses → the WHOLE delta is rejected.
- WRONG (role flattened onto speaker0): `add: [{{"subject":"speaker0","predicate":"held_role","object":"Senior Engineer",...}}, {{"subject":"speaker0","predicate":"worked_at","object":"Org_1",...}}, {{"subject":"speaker0","predicate":"start_date","object":"Jan 2014",...}}, {{"subject":"speaker0","predicate":"end_date","object":"Sep 2017",...}}]` — co-temporal attributes orphan from the role. With multiple roles in one transcript the dates cannot be paired back to a title; downstream reasoning over "what title did speaker0 hold in 2015?" fails.

Existing bare anonymizer placeholders (`Person_1`, `City_1`, `Org_1`, etc.) stay bare. Do NOT re-brace them. Leave them exactly as the extractor emitted them.

Before returning: every `{{Prefix_N}}` you wrote in `add`/`modify` MUST have a matching `bindings` entry (key without braces) whose value is an exact transcript span — a descriptor phrase ("my father", "the workshop last spring") counts as an exact span, not only a proper name. If you cannot bind it to ANY exact transcript span, do NOT introduce the placeholder — write the entity surface inline instead. A single unbound placeholder rejects the WHOLE delta, not just that one fact — everything you wrote in `add`/`modify` this turn reverts, and the pre-enrichment facts are kept unchanged.

## Symmetric predicate canonicalization

Symmetric predicates (one triple represents both directions; lexicographic-min subject wins): friend_of, friends_with, married_to, spouse_of, sibling_of, brother_of, sister_of, cousin_of (when reciprocal), colleague_of, coworker_of, neighbor_of, partner_of, related_to, met_with, knows (only if reciprocal), agrees_with, disagrees_with, attended_with, shares_interest_with.

Few-shot — input: `[2] friend_of(A, B)` AND `[3] friend_of(B, A)`
→ drop: `[3]`. Keep `[2]` (lexicographic-min subject).

Few-shot — input: `[4] parent_of(A, B)` AND `[5] child_of(B, A)`
→ no delta — asymmetric pair (also: manages/reports_to, taught/studied_under, likes/liked_by) — distinct semantics, keep both directions.


## Predicate de-duplication

Per `(subject, object)` pair, emit AT MOST ONE predicate.

Few-shot — input: `[6] worked_for(P, Org_1)` AND `[7] employed_by(P, Org_1)`
→ drop: `[7]` (synonym of `[6]`; pick the predicate already most frequent in the rest of your output).

Few-shot — input: `[8] parent_of(P, Q)` AND `[9] birth_parent_of(P, Q)`
→ drop: `[8]` (more specific predicate `[9]` wins; same relation, keep the specific surface).
